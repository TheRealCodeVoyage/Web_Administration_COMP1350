# Online Activity 6: PKI & Proxy Recap

*COMP 1350 — Web Administration — distributed Week 5, due before Week 6*

## Why This Matters

Week 6 combines your Week 5 certificate with a reverse proxy sitting in front of your Week 3 Node app — the architectural payoff of everything built so far this term. This activity previews the one concept you haven't seen yet: what a proxy actually is.

## Before Class: Read

- [Cloudflare Learning Center — What is a reverse proxy?](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/cdn/glossary/reverse-proxy/) — short glossary-style page
- [Cloudflare Learning Center — Forward proxy vs. reverse proxy](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/cdn/glossary/reverse-proxy/) — same page covers both; read the comparison section
- [Cloudflare Learning Center — CDN SSL/TLS security](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/cdn/cdn-ssl-tls-security/) — skim the "TLS termination" concept, even though this page is framed around CDNs

## Come to Class Ready to Answer

1. In one sentence each: what does a forward proxy do, and what does a reverse proxy do? Who is each one acting on behalf of?
2. If a reverse proxy "terminates TLS," what exactly does that mean happens to the encrypted connection at that point?
3. Why would a company put a reverse proxy in front of their application server instead of just exposing the application server directly to the internet? Name two reasons.
4. A root Certificate Authority signs an intermediate CA, which signs your server's certificate. Why do you think real-world PKI uses this multi-level "chain" instead of having the root CA sign every server certificate directly?

## How This Feeds Forward

- These questions form part of **Quiz 6**, given in Week 6.
- Week 6's lecture and lab (which is also **Milestone A** of your group project) have you configure Nginx as a reverse proxy terminating TLS in front of your own Node/Express app.
