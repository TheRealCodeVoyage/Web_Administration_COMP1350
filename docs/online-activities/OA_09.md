# Online Activity 9: SPOF & High Availability Recap

*COMP 1350 — Web Administration — distributed Week 9, due before Week 10*

## Why This Matters

Week 9 solved one problem (overloaded servers) but quietly created another. Week 10 exposes it directly: your load balancer is now the only thing standing between users and your entire system. This activity gets you to spot that problem yourself, before the lecture reveals it.

## Before Class: Read

- [Wikipedia — Single point of failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_point_of_failure) — read the definition and the "avoidance" section
- [Cloudflare Learning Center — What is redundancy?](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/network-layer/what-is-redundancy/) — short page on redundancy as a design principle
- [Keepalived project — About](https://www.keepalived.org/) — skim the homepage; you're not expected to understand VRRP yet, just to know the tool exists

## Come to Class Ready to Answer

1. In your own words, what is a "single point of failure"? Give an example from *outside* computing (e.g., transportation, electricity) before giving a technical one.
2. Look back at the architecture you built in Week 9 (two backend servers behind one load balancer). Where is the SPOF in that diagram now?
3. If you added a second load balancer to fix that, how would traffic actually reach it if it fails over — does the IP address change, or does something else happen? (Take your best guess; this is exactly what Week 10 explains.)
4. Redundancy costs money (a second server, a second load balancer, etc.). How would you, as a system owner, decide how much redundancy is "enough" for a given application?

## How This Feeds Forward

- These questions form part of **Quiz 9**, given in Week 10.
- Week 10's lecture and lab (which is also **Milestone C** groundwork) add a second HAProxy instance and introduce keepalived/VRRP to answer question 3 for real.
